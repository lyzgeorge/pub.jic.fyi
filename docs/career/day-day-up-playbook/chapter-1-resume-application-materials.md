# Chapter 1 — Resume & Application Materials

> **Source:** Condensed from *The Day Day Up Career Playbook* (concise edition), based on Jonathan Lee's career-coaching videos.

Make your relevant value easy to find, then use several routes to reach the people hiring.



![Impact bullet: turn a duty into measurable business impact](/assets/career/day-day-up-playbook/chapter-1/01-comparison-impact-bullet.png)
## 1.1 Write impact bullets

A responsibility says what the job involved. An impact bullet explains what changed because of your work.

**Use it:** For each relevant role, ask: “If I did this well, why was my manager happy?” Connect the answer to revenue, cost, time, quality, or capacity. Write **problem → action and skill → result**. Use the target job description’s terminology where it accurately describes your experience.

**Practical example from the source material:**

> **Before:** Performed month-end reconciliations to verify accuracy and completeness of financial records.
>
> **After:** Redesigned reconciliation workflows to solve month-end close delays as transaction volumes scaled. Reduced close time by two days, supporting rapid growth without additional headcount.

The improvement is the business consequence, not simply the added number. Keep the bullet short enough to scan, usually about two lines.

**Check before sending:** Can you explain your contribution, how the result was measured, and what would have happened without it? Use estimates only when you can explain their basis; do not invent achievements or copy the example’s metrics.

## 1.2 Make the resume easy to scan

- Use a simple, single-column layout, readable text, and clear section headings. The coach recommends one page for candidates with fewer than roughly ten years of experience; use this as a default, not a universal limit.
- Put your strongest relevant evidence near the top. Give work experience more space than unrelated projects.
- Skip generic summary paragraphs. If a summary helps, make it one specific line: **[role] with [experience], using [strength] to deliver [relevant outcome].**
- Put education near the top when it is your strongest recent qualification; otherwise lead with experience.
- Remove irrelevant history and optional location details that distract from fit. Use official English company names and explain unfamiliar tools by function. Do not rename a tool you used as a different product.
- Include skills you can substantiate. For a missing skill, learn it, produce evidence, then describe your actual proficiency.
- Use a professional filename and check the exported document, not just the editor view.

**Quick check:** Ask someone to skim the resume and tell you the role you suit and your strongest achievement. If either is unclear, fix the top third first.

## 1.3 Fill an experience gap with visible work

Use a relevant side project, freelance assignment, or company-designed simulation to demonstrate a missing capability. The corpus recommends Forage simulations and freelance work as accessible starting points.

1. Pick a project that matches a priority in your target job description.
2. Complete a tangible deliverable you can explain or show.
3. Describe the problem, tools, and output. Keep simulations in a clearly labeled Projects section; do not present them as employment or real commercial results.

**Resume pattern:**

> [Program name] — Job simulation: used [tools] to analyze [problem] and produced [deliverable].

A recognizable brand is secondary to being able to explain what you actually did.

## 1.4 Decide where to apply and how to tailor

Treat a job description as a starting point for understanding the manager’s needs. Early requirements and unusually specific wording can reveal priorities; confirm them in conversation.

**Before applying:**

1. Identify the main business problem and the capabilities needed to solve it.
2. Separate essential eligibility or technical requirements from preferences.
3. Match your strongest evidence to the most important requirements.
4. Tailor the opening summary and relevant bullets; keep the facts unchanged.

The coach’s “one-third match” rule is encouragement against self-rejection, not a reliable hiring threshold. Apply when you have a credible case for the core work. Changing either role or industry generally gives you an easier relevance story than changing both at once.

**Example:** An accountant moving from banking into technology can retain the accounting role while learning the industry. A move into both a new industry and a new function needs more evidence of transferable skills.

## 1.5 Use four search channels

| Channel | Action | What makes it useful |
|---|---|---|
| Applications | Save focused searches; review recent relevant listings regularly. | Tailored evidence reaches an open role. |
| Referrals | Give a contact a short fit summary they can forward. | They can explain why the manager should interview you. |
| Direct outreach | Contact a relevant decision maker with a specific value proposition. | The message connects your experience to their problem. |
| Public work | Share a useful project, analysis, or lesson. | People can see your capability before speaking with you. |

If applications consume nearly all your time without producing conversations, shift some time into the other channels. The coach suggests reviewing this allocation every twenty days and moving roughly 20% of effort; treat that as a review cadence, not a success formula.

**Cold message, adapted:**

> Hi [name], I help [type of team] solve [problem], most recently by [relevant result]. I noticed [specific company development or role need]. Would a short conversation about [specific contribution] be useful?

Keep platform tactics secondary. Filters and interfaces change; relevance and a credible reason to respond matter more than a particular LinkedIn URL trick.

## 1.6 Keep useful relationships open

After a substantive interview process, reply to a rejection with appreciation and continued interest. Ask whether a later check-in would be welcome. Respect silence or a refusal.

> Thanks for letting me know. I enjoyed learning about [specific work] and remain interested in the team. Would you be open to me checking in if a similar role opens?

When a suitable opening appears, reply on the existing thread with the role and any meaningful update to your experience. See Chapter 4 for interview follow-up.

**Ready to apply:** You have a readable resume, defensible impact bullets, a clear match to the role’s core needs, and a search plan that reaches people through more than one channel.
