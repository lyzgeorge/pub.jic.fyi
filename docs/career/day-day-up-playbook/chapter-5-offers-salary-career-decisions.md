# Chapter 5 — Offers, Salary & Career Decisions

> **Source:** Condensed from *The Day Day Up Career Playbook* (concise edition), based on Jonathan Lee's career-coaching videos.

Understand the range, evaluate the whole offer, and make specific requests before accepting. The coach favors active negotiation; outcomes still depend on the employer, role, budget, and your alternatives.

## 5.1 Establish compensation alignment early

When asked for expectations, first seek the employer’s range:

> I’m still learning about the scope and mutual fit. Could you share the range budgeted for this role? I can tell you whether we’re in the same ballpark.

If no range is offered, ask about the level or range for comparable roles. If they still need your number, give a considered range with scope for refinement:

> Based on what I understand so far, I’m considering [X–Y]. I’d want to refine that once the responsibilities and total package are clear.

If their range works, say so. If it does not, clarify the gap rather than continuing on a false assumption. Do not claim competing offers or interview progress you do not have.

## 5.2 Negotiate before saying yes

Express enthusiasm without treating an initial figure as final. Separate three stages:

1. **Initial discussion:** Establish whether the range is broadly workable.
2. **Verbal offer:** Clarify scope and package, then make your request.
3. **Written offer:** Verify every agreed term and resolve remaining questions before accepting.

**Request, adapted:**

> I’m excited about the role. Given [relevant experience], [scope], and [supporting market information], I’d be comfortable signing at [amount]. Is there flexibility to get there?

Use a conditional close only when you mean it:

> If we can agree on [specific term], I’m ready to accept.

A competing offer or a good current role gives you alternatives. Explain those truthfully and selectively; you do not need to disclose every detail. If base pay is fixed, explore other terms that matter to you. The corpus mentions title, level, signing incentives, vacation, and other benefits.

Do not assume a standard 10% cushion or zero risk. Ask respectfully, evaluate the response, and decide whether the remaining difference matters enough to decline.

## 5.3 Read the entire offer

| Area | Verify before accepting |
|---|---|
| Title and level | Match to the advertised role, internal level, responsibilities, and reporting line. |
| Guaranteed pay | Base salary, payment terms, and any guaranteed amounts. |
| Variable compensation | Bonus conditions, equity terms, vesting, and what is uncertain. |
| Repayment obligations | Clawbacks or conditions attached to signing or relocation payments. |
| Benefits | Leave, insurance, retirement contributions, and benefits relevant to you. |
| Working arrangement | Location, flexibility, expected hours, and travel where relevant. |
| Future promises | Whether promotion or compensation commitments are actually documented. |

Separate guaranteed compensation from potential upside. Ask about mismatches rather than assuming the recruiter’s explanation will appear in the final contract.

## 5.4 Compare offers against your priorities

The coach often prioritizes title between comparable companies because it can affect future opportunities. Treat that as his preference, not a rule for every career.

Compare each offer on:

- The work and skills you will build.
- Scope, level, and credibility of the title.
- Guaranteed compensation and uncertain upside.
- Manager support, autonomy, and team conditions.
- Sustainability for your health and personal commitments.
- What could change after a reorganization.

Investigate the manager with concrete questions about delegation and development. Where appropriate, ask former reports what helped people succeed. Hesitation or vague answers warrant follow-up; they are not proof of a bad manager.

**Decision standard:** Be able to name the main benefit, the trade-off you are accepting, and the unanswered question that could change your choice.

## 5.5 Reply clearly

| Situation | Practical reply |
|---|---|
| Negotiate | “Thank you for the offer. I’m excited about [work]. Based on [reason], could we discuss [specific change]?” |
| Another process is pending | Tell the other employer you have a real deadline and ask whether they can accelerate. Ask the offering employer for an extension if needed; do not assume one. |
| Decline | “Thank you for the time and offer. After considering my priorities, I’ve decided to pursue another direction. I appreciated [specific aspect] and hope we can stay connected.” |
| Accept | “Thank you. I’m happy to accept the offer on the agreed terms and look forward to joining on [date]. Please let me know the next steps.” |

**Before sending an acceptance:** Resolve your important questions, check the final written terms, and make sure your wording reflects the decision you actually intend.
