# Academic Integrity: Citation, Similarity, and Reference Management

> **Original title:** Academic Integrity
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=192440d4-6bf2-4f72-ae2f-b4b700eb74d9)

Academic integrity means making the origin of borrowed words, images, music, ideas, and evidence visible. Research necessarily builds on earlier work; citation lets readers locate sources, check whether they have been represented fairly, and distinguish prior contributions from the writer's own.

## Plagiarism concerns attribution, not only intent

Plagiarism is presenting another person's work or idea as one's own. At Trinity, consequences can follow the act even when there was no intention to deceive. Copying, rearranging copied paragraphs, or substituting words does not solve the attribution problem. Reusing one's own earlier assignment for another class also requires acknowledgement.

Short paraphrases can demonstrate synthesis, but the source must still be cited. Long stretches of paraphrase may also weaken the writer's own analysis. Direct quotations require quotation marks or a suitable block format and attribution; block quotations should be used sparingly. Common knowledge does not normally need citation, but a distinctive claim, interpretation, wording, quotation, dataset, image, or idea does.

## A similarity score is evidence to inspect

Turnitin compares a submission with websites, publications, books, and student papers. Its score reports textual similarity, not a verdict about plagiarism.

![Turnitin slide explaining that a high score can be acceptable with citations and a low score can conceal uncited paraphrase](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/academic-integrity/similarity-score.jpg)

A high score may be legitimate when matches are quoted and cited; a low score may coexist with extensive uncited paraphrase. Interpretation therefore requires examining each match, the type of material, and its attribution. Lecturers also notice abrupt changes in style, terminology, or accuracy.

## Use the required citation style consistently

A citation style governs which bibliographic elements appear, their order and punctuation, and how citations connect the text to full references. The school, department, course director, or supervisor decides which style to use.

![Three broad citation-style families: parenthetical or inline, numbered, and footnotes](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/academic-integrity/citation-style-families.jpg)

- **Parenthetical/inline styles** give brief details in the text, commonly author and date, with an alphabetical reference list. APA is an example; MLA commonly uses author and page.
- **Numbered styles** assign references numbers in order of first mention and reuse the same number for the same source. Vancouver is a common example.
- **Footnote styles** place an ascending note number in the text and fuller details at the bottom of the page; Chicago Notes and Bibliography is a prominent example.

The same source looks different in each system. Consistency includes capitalization, italics, author order, page ranges, DOI presentation, and punctuation—not just the in-text marker.

## Generative AI requires permission and acknowledgement

The recording says AI use may be allowed unless a school, department, or lecturer prohibits or limits it. This is not blanket permission: instructions for the specific assessment govern. Permitted use must be acknowledged, and some styles may also require a reference-list entry. Check current Trinity and local course guidance because policy can change. The existence of a tool does not transfer responsibility for accuracy, originality, privacy, or compliance.

## Keep records while researching

Record source details at the moment of discovery. Save catalogue records, database searches, alerts, notes, page numbers, and links. This prevents the late-stage problem of reconstructing where a claim originated.

![Reference-management tools such as EndNote, Zotero, and Mendeley importing records and inserting formatted citations](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/academic-integrity/reference-management.jpg)

Reference managers centralize records, import metadata from catalogues and databases, and insert citations into word-processing documents. They reduce formatting labour but do not guarantee correct metadata or appropriate citation. Check imported records and the final bibliography. Trinity Library supports EndNote and offers training; subject librarians can advise on searching and reference workflows.

## Submission checklist

1. Complete Trinity's Ready Steady Write tutorial and required declaration.
2. Identify the style required by the programme or assessment.
3. Cite borrowed ideas as well as copied words.
4. Mark quotations clearly and keep paraphrases genuinely in your own structure and language.
5. Review every similarity match rather than targeting a score.
6. Confirm whether and how AI may be used, then acknowledge it as required.
7. Verify every reference against the source before submission.
