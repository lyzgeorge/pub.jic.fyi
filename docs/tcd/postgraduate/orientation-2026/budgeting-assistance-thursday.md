# Budgeting and the Postgraduate Student Assistance Fund

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Budgeting and the Student Assistance Fund (Thursday)
>
> **Session date:** 10 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=bd1c78c7-1bae-4a9f-b019-b4ba00b48dc6)

A budget is a plan for allocating limited income across essential, discretionary, irregular, and emergency costs. It is personal: childcare, medication, transport, cultural obligations, accessibility, safety, or social connection can change whether an expense functions as a need.

## Build a realistic baseline

Start with predictable income and fixed obligations, then estimate variable essentials, study costs, discretionary spending, annual or one-off expenses, and an emergency margin. Use bank statements rather than memory. Convert infrequent costs into monthly amounts so that insurance, travel, deposits, replacement devices, or professional fees do not appear as surprises.

Distinguishing needs from wants is useful only when it supports priorities. It should not become moral judgement or remove every source of enjoyment. **Crash budgeting**—an extreme short-term restriction that cannot be sustained—often produces rebound spending, shame, and avoidance. A workable plan leaves modest flexibility and is revised when assumptions prove wrong.

![Slide linking to free MABS guidance and a Senior Tutor budget tool](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/budgeting-assistance-thursday/creating-a-budget.jpg)

The session recommends the free, confidential Money Advice and Budgeting Service (MABS) and Trinity’s budgeting tool. Seeking advice is preferable to trying to solve serious debt or shortfall alone.

## Reduce costs deliberately

Potential savings include planning meals, shopping with a list, cooking in groups, comparing unit prices, using lower-cost supermarkets, and avoiding purchases driven by lack of preparation. Active travel and the correct Leap Card category may reduce transport costs. Student discounts, campus services, societies, galleries, museums, and university events can provide lower-cost social and cultural activity.

Safety remains a legitimate need. Budget enough to travel home safely rather than constructing a plan that only works if no unexpected late journey occurs. Discounts, rates, card systems, and technical availability change and should be checked before preloading money or relying on them.

## Student Assistance Fund

The **Student Assistance Fund (SAF)** is means-tested support for registered students experiencing financial difficulty. It supplements other support, including SUSI where applicable, but the session states that it normally cannot pay tuition fees. Funding is limited, awards are not immediate, and application rules, dates, thresholds, and typical amounts change each year.

![Slide outlining the PG-SAF application process](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/budgeting-assistance-thursday/pg-saf-application.jpg)

The application is not assessed until all required documentation is supplied.

An application combines:

1. an online form during the published window;
2. core financial evidence, such as income records and bank statements;
3. evidence of relevant circumstances, such as rent, childcare, medical costs, essential travel, or another documented pressure.

![Slide listing core documents and evidence of circumstances for PG-SAF](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/budgeting-assistance-thursday/pg-saf-documentation.jpg)

Only provide what is requested, but ensure names and amounts are visible and documents are digital and legible. Assessment cannot finish until required evidence is complete. Start collecting records early, especially where documents must come from another country or agency.

SAF is generally a one-off payment based on the assessed situation, sometimes with additions for particular circumstances. A later emergency should still be reported even if the student already applied or missed the first window.

## Other assistance routes

Benefactions and bursaries have distinct criteria linked to course, background, activity, or circumstance. Students receiving SAF or relying solely on social welfare may be eligible for waivers or refunds of certain student levies under the applicable rules. Student parents may have additional support. The Students’ Union may offer small short-term loans or food assistance.

These schemes are not interchangeable. Check current eligibility, closing dates, whether repayment is required, and whether support can cover fees, living costs, or only a defined expense.

## Action checklist

1. Build the budget from real transactions and include irregular costs.
2. Preserve a small sustainable allowance rather than crash budgeting.
3. Review subscriptions, food planning, transport category, and student discounts.
4. Contact MABS or student support before debt becomes unmanageable.
5. Confirm current SAF registration, eligibility, dates, and exclusions.
6. Gather income, bank, rent, childcare, medical, and other evidence early.
7. Search benefactions, levy waivers, parent support, and Students’ Union assistance separately.
8. Report emergencies even when they occur outside the expected plan.
