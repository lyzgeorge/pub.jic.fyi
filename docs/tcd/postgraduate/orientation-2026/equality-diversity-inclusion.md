# Equality, Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion at Trinity

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Equality, Diversity and Inclusion
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=d9a02742-0f1c-405d-8320-b4ba00b2b0f4)

Equality, diversity, inclusion, and equity describe related but distinct parts of a university community. Together they address who can enter Trinity, whether different people can participate and succeed, how the institution adapts to their needs, and what support is required to produce fair outcomes.

## Four connected concepts

- **Equality** concerns equal opportunity and protection, including access to study or work and the quality of the resulting experience.
- **Diversity** means that the community includes a wide range of backgrounds, experiences, identities, and contributions.
- **Inclusion** means participation is meaningful rather than a numerical or procedural checkbox; the institution changes practices where necessary.
- **Equity** recognises that different starting conditions and structural barriers may require different forms or levels of support to reach a fair outcome.

For example, additional financial support for a student from a socioeconomically disadvantaged background is not preferential treatment in this model. It addresses a pre-existing barrier so that opportunity becomes more comparable.

## Legal and institutional foundations

The session describes EDI as a legal and institutional obligation, a contributor to academic and organisational success, and an ethical commitment. Irish equality law includes the Equal Status Acts and Employment Equality Acts. Trinity's statutes refer to liberty, equality, equity, and justice, while its 2025–30 strategy commits the university to eliminating discrimination, promoting equality of opportunity, protecting human rights, and embedding inclusion.

Policies turn these commitments into practical expectations: what a person is entitled to, who is responsible, how support is requested, and how an issue may be escalated.

## Protected grounds and broader disadvantage

![Slide listing the nine Equal Status grounds and noting socioeconomic status as an additional cross-cutting concern](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/equality-diversity-inclusion/equal-status-grounds.jpg)

The nine grounds presented from the Equal Status framework are gender, marital status, family status, age, disability, sexual orientation, race, religion, and membership of the Traveller community. These are broad categories: gender includes gender identity and expression; disability may include physical, intellectual, learning, neurodivergent, medical, and some mental-health conditions; race includes ethnicity, nationality, and skin colour; religion includes faith and no faith.

The recording separately highlights socioeconomic status. It was not presented as a statutory tenth ground, but as a major influence that intersects with the protected grounds and affects participation.

## Intersectionality

Intersectionality examines how multiple identities and social conditions combine to create an experience that cannot always be understood by considering each category separately.

![Intersectionality wheel showing interacting dimensions of identity, privilege, marginalisation, and power](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/equality-diversity-inclusion/intersectionality.jpg)

A disabled woman, for example, may encounter a barrier specific to the interaction of disability and gender rather than two independent problems. Relevant circumstances can also include language, housing, transport, education, employment, family situation, or location. These conditions change over time, so support should respond to the whole person rather than treat identity as fixed.

## EDI across the distributed university

Trinity is not only the historic city-centre campus. Teaching, research, placement, services, and work occur across hospitals, specialist facilities, sports grounds, Belfast, Birr, and other locations.

![Map illustrating Trinity as a distributed campus across academic, health, sports, living, and specialist sites](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/equality-diversity-inclusion/distributed-campus.jpg)

An inclusive service therefore cannot rely only on a person walking into a central office. A student on a long clinical placement may still need accessible information, a faith space, breastfeeding facilities, disability support, or a route to advice. The same principles apply across academic schools and professional services such as Registry, catering, and sport.

The Equality Policy described in the session is intended to cover the full Trinity community: students, staff, guest lecturers, relevant alumni interactions, temporary or sessional workers, contractors, consultants, service users, visitors, and associated bodies. Students employed by Trinity may be covered both as students and, for that work, as staff.

## Policy and support structure

Key policy areas identified in the recording include:

- the overarching Equality Policy;
- Dignity and Respect and Sexual Misconduct policies;
- support for student parents, student carers, and students experiencing pregnancy;
- gender identity and gender expression;
- the Race Equality Action Plan;
- accessible information, reasonable accommodation, and Irish Sign Language procedures.

Policy wording and approval status can change. Consult the current Trinity Policy Hub rather than relying on the 2026 slide alone.

The EDI Office coordinates university-wide work and helps translate a situation into practical next steps or the correct referral. Academic schools have Directors of EDI, while professional-service areas have EDI leads. Other routes include the Dignity, Respect and Consent Service, disAbility Service, Students' Union, Postgraduate Advisory Service, tutors, academic schools, HR partners, societies, sports clubs, and community organisations.

The Dignity, Respect and Consent Service is the specialist route for harassment, discrimination, misconduct, or sexual harassment. It can support an initial conversation and explain informal or formal options, including complaint and resolution processes.

![Slide summarising EDI contacts, policy resources, training, consultations, and the equality office email](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/equality-diversity-inclusion/support-resources.jpg)

## Using the support system

1. Describe the practical barrier, concern, or intended activity, even if you do not know which policy applies.
2. Check the EDI website and current Policy Hub for rights, responsibilities, contacts, and escalation routes.
3. For a course-specific question, contact the School's Director of EDI or relevant local lead.
4. Contact the EDI Office when the correct route is unclear or advice needs to span several services.
5. Use the Dignity, Respect and Consent Service for harassment, discrimination, misconduct, sexual harassment, or disclosure support.
6. Ask for reasonable accommodation or accessible information through the relevant process rather than assuming one standard solution fits everyone.
7. Keep records of requests, responses, agreed action, and policy references where an issue requires follow-up.

## Learning and participation

Students can develop practical skills through EDI in Higher Education, race-equality, unconscious-bias, Dignity and Respect, bystander, and disclosure training, as well as LinkedIn Learning and self-directed resources. Societies, sports clubs, Students' Union roles, community events, and EDI consultations offer participation beyond formal training.

Calls for student input may be circulated through academic schools or university-wide announcements. Students with a specific interest can also contact the EDI Office directly for guidance on relevant groups, events, or projects.
