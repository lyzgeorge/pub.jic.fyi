# Postgraduate Fees, Sponsorship, and Student Finance

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Fees and Finance with the Student Finance Team
>
> **Session date:** 8 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=e4308635-ba30-424a-8ed5-b4ba00af8275)

Student Finance within Academic Registry raises fee invoices, receives payments, administers sponsorship records, processes eligible refunds and student payments, and supports US federal and private-loan administration. Dates, instalments, charges, funding amounts, and programme eligibility are academic-year specific; verify them in the current portal and Student Finance guidance.

## Fees form part of registration

When invited to register, students complete financial tasks in the my.tcd.ie portal. The account should show tuition, levies or other charges, and any recorded sponsorship. Registration normally requires the first amount due to be paid or covered by an accepted sponsor record.

For 2026/27, the session described a three-instalment arrangement: an initial 50% of tuition plus relevant levies, followed by two 25% tuition instalments. Those percentages and dates are historical source facts, not standing rules.

## Sponsorship changes who is invoiced—not ultimate responsibility

A public body, employer, scholarship provider, US loan, or private sponsor may cover some or all fees. The student must submit the required evidence during registration so Student Finance can transfer the appropriate liability to the sponsor.

Check scope carefully: a sponsor may cover tuition but not levies or other charges. The recording emphasises that fee liability ultimately remains with the student if a sponsor does not pay, reduces support, or becomes ineligible. Monitor the account and respond to discrepancies early.

If a student paid in advance and sponsorship is later applied, Student Finance may process an eligible refund. Follow the published refund procedure rather than assuming a credit will be returned automatically.

## Pay through the supported route

Use the payment method presented for the fee category and location. The session described portal card payment for domestic categories and TransferMate options for international payments, with direct EFT handled exceptionally. Supported routes improve matching and reduce delays.

Always include the eight-digit Trinity student number in payments and enquiries. Names are not unique; the identifier allows Finance to match money and correspondence to the correct account. Keep confirmation of every transaction.

## Enquiries and unresolved registration

Many questions—payment methods, instalments, sponsorship, SUSI, refunds, and federal aid—are covered on the Student Finance website. If the published material does not resolve the case, submit an Academic Registry/Student Finance enquiry and include the student number plus relevant documentation. The service desk can help where a finance or registration problem blocks access to the student account or timetable.

Students facing affordability difficulties can also seek advice from relevant student-support routes; this is separate from assuming that Student Finance can create an individual payment plan.

## US federal and private aid

US federal-aid eligibility is determined under US rules and requires the relevant federal application and identity requirements. Trinity administers eligible funding but does not decide federal eligibility. Programme restrictions can apply—for example to non-degree study abroad, certificates or diplomas, online provision, nursing outside the US, or certain joint arrangements—and can change.

Private loans may be available where federal aid is unavailable, but terms differ. Students should use the current Trinity federal-aid pages, confirm programme eligibility, submit documents early, and review the cost-of-attendance calculation and disbursement timetable.

## Checklist

1. Review the portal invoice line by line before accepting fees.
2. Verify current instalment dates and registration deadline.
3. Upload sponsor evidence and check exactly which charges it covers.
4. Treat unpaid sponsor amounts as a potential personal liability.
5. Pay through the supported route and retain receipts.
6. Put the eight-digit student number on every payment and enquiry.
7. Check current programme eligibility for US aid.
8. Raise discrepancies before they block registration or generate overdue balances.
