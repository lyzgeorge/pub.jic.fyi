# Innovation and Entrepreneurship at Trinity

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Innovation and Entrepreneurship in Trinity
>
> **Session date:** 10 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=4451453c-108d-4471-a71b-b4ba00b5d25a)

Innovation is the process of turning a question, discovery or new approach into practical impact. Trinity Innovation Hub is designed as a shared access point where postgraduate researchers, academics, ventures, industry, investors, professional advisers and non-profit organisations can meet and develop that impact.

## A place for multidisciplinary work

The Hub is at Trinity East, roughly a ten-minute walk from the main campus. The recording describes a five-and-a-half-acre site whose Innovation Hub was due to open officially at the end of November 2026, with the Laidlaw Library planned for late 2027. Because dates and access arrangements can change, visitors should confirm current details.

The physical space matters because many research and enterprise problems require collaboration across disciplines. Postgraduate students were invited to visit and arrange free hot-desking through the Hub team. Events and shared workspace are intended to make informal encounters and sustained partnerships easier.

## The innovation ecosystem

The Hub is not simply office space. Industry participants enter in order to collaborate with Trinity academics, protecting the site's partnership purpose. Its community includes researchers, spin-outs and start-ups, investors, professional-service firms, social enterprises and non-profits. This mix supports both commercial outcomes and social impact.

The Hub is also distinct from a Local Enterprise Office (LEO). It cooperates with LEOs and national supports as part of the wider ecosystem, but is not formally part of them.

## Forms of support

Support begins with the need rather than a single programme:

- **Research collaboration:** introductions and structures for academics and external partners to work together.
- **Consult:** a managed route through which an organisation can commission academic research and receive help with the relationship.
- **Knowledge transfer:** specialist case managers help identify intellectual property, understand protection options and move knowledge toward application.
- **New ventures:** pitching sessions, accelerator activity and introductions can connect suitable ventures with investors and advisers.
- **Talent and education:** organisations can engage with postgraduate and PhD talent, while students can access programmes, events and multidisciplinary networks.

These routes overlap. A research question may develop into protected intellectual property, a partnership, a social enterprise or a commercial venture; not every project must follow the same sequence.

## A practical route for postgraduate students

1. Define the problem or repeated practice you want to question.
2. Identify what is missing: another discipline, industry context, research commissioning, IP advice, venture support, funding contact or workspace.
3. Contact the Innovation Hub and describe the need rather than assuming a particular programme.
4. Visit the Trinity East space and use an event or hot-desk day to test whether the community is useful.
5. Before disclosing a potentially valuable invention, seek appropriate knowledge-transfer or IP guidance.
6. Confirm all current programmes, dates, occupancy and access arrangements with the Hub.
