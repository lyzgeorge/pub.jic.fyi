# Being LGBTQ+ at Trinity and in Dublin

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Being LGBTQ+ in Trinity & Dublin
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=8ebeb230-4911-4892-bd56-b4ba00b34ab8)

LGBTQ+ life at Trinity is supported through policy, academic networks, student-led communities, health and advisory services, and links to organisations across Dublin. These routes serve different needs: formal protection, research community, low-pressure social contact, individual advice, and specialist support.

## Rights and institutional protection

Gender identity and sexual orientation are protected grounds in Ireland. The session placed two major 2015 changes in context: marriage equality was approved by referendum, and the Gender Recognition Act established legal recognition based on self-declaration. It also identified Trinity policies on gender identity and expression, equality, dignity and respect, and sexual misconduct.

Policies become practically relevant when discrimination, bullying, harassment, or assault occurs. Students can seek clarification from the EDI Office and support from the Dignity, Respect and Consent team rather than having to interpret procedures alone.

## Academic belonging: Pride in Research

**Pride in Research** is a volunteer-led initiative for LGBTQ+ researchers at every career stage and allies whose research concerns LGBTQ+ communities. Its annual conference during Pride Month combines two forms of visibility:

- research on queer lives, culture, health, history, and communities;
- research on any subject conducted by LGBTQ+ scholars.

That distinction matters because inclusion is not limited to people whose topic is sexuality or gender. The network also creates peer contact, role models, and opportunities to build new events. It began when postgraduate researchers obtained Equality Fund support for a conference; the continuing programme demonstrates how students can turn an identified gap into durable academic infrastructure.

## Finding community through QSoc

QSoc, founded in 1983, is described as Ireland’s oldest LGBTQ+ student society. It offers ordinary social events, daily coffee hours, and identity-specific **Closed Spaces**. A Closed Space is confidential and limited to people who hold—or are questioning—the identity under discussion, so participants can compare experiences without needing to explain the basic context.

The society’s inclusion scheme reduces the barrier of attending alone. A student can contact the Inclusions Officer, arrange a conversation or coffee beforehand, and enter an event already knowing someone. This is especially useful for newcomers, international students, postgraduates, and anyone still deciding how openly they wish to participate.

## Student-led advocacy and material support

TCDSU routes include the President for campaigns, the Graduate Officer for postgraduate concerns, the Welfare and Equality Officer for personal welfare matters, and the part-time LGBT+ Rights Officer. Students can participate through open campaign meetings, campaign communications, class representation, or year representation for postgraduate researchers.

The union and QSoc also administer the **T Fund** for gender-affirming non-medical items such as binders, gaffs, clothing, and makeup. The source specified two application rounds each year, a €1,000 allocation per term, and a limit of €200 per student. Medical supplies are excluded by the fund’s rules, and current application details should be checked in the weekly union email.

## Changing a name or gender on the student record

Trinity’s process does not require a student to complete legal transition before asking the university to use a different name or gender marker.

![Two routes for changing a name or gender on a Trinity student record, depending on whether supporting documentation is available](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/lgbtq-trinity-dublin/updating-student-record.jpg)

The slide distinguishes two routes:

- **With a gender-recognition certificate or other supporting document:** complete the Change of Details form and bring it, the evidence, and the student ID card to Academic Registry.
- **Without supporting documentation:** complete the form, meet the Postgraduate Advisory Service (PAS), have PAS co-sign it, and submit it with the student ID card to Academic Registry.

The PAS meeting is not an assessment of identity. Its purpose is to make sure the student understands where the changed details will appear—for example, in correspondence or on the graduation parchment. The source stated that Academic Registry issues a replacement card in the new name without a charge.

## A map of Trinity support

Different needs belong with different services; no single office covers everything.

![Trinity LGBTQ+ support routes for counselling, healthcare, equality, intersectional peer support, misconduct, and legal advice](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/lgbtq-trinity-dublin/lgbtq-support-routes.jpg)

- **Student Counselling Service:** confidential space to discuss identity, coming out, questioning, or personal difficulties.
- **College Health:** sexual-health and PrEP clinics; the session described STI care as confidential and non-judgemental.
- **EDI Office:** clarification on equality policy and institutional responsibilities.
- **Dignity, Respect and Consent team:** support after bullying, harassment, or assault.
- **Trinity FLAC:** free legal advice from qualified solicitors, including on equality, immigration, and entitlements.
- **Queer Disabled Peer Group:** an intersectional staff–student group meeting every two weeks during term; registration with Disability Service was not required.

## Safer participation in Dublin nightlife

The session described Dublin as generally safe while stressing that it remains a capital city. Practical preparation matters more than assuming a venue or neighbourhood is risk-free:

1. Decide how to get home before going out and keep a backup when late-night transport fails.
2. Tell a friend where you are going, especially when visiting someone’s home, and arrange a check-in.
3. Leave when a situation feels wrong; social pressure is not evidence of safety.
4. Use the free condoms, lubricant, and dental dams available from TCDSU or QSoc, and use confidential STI testing when needed.
5. Do not take an unidentified substance or try one for the first time in an uncontrolled setting; tell someone what was taken.
6. Replace an unattended or altered drink. Unexpected intoxication or breathing difficulty requires immediate help.
7. If needle spiking is suspected, seek medical assistance and report it promptly rather than remaining alone.

## Community beyond Trinity

Dublin offers queer bars, club nights, sports teams, running and hiking groups, book clubs, arts festivals, and alcohol-free communities. Programmes and venues change, so the durable lesson is to choose a format that matches the kind of contact wanted: regular sport, quiet conversation, identity-specific peer support, arts participation, activism, or nightlife.

**Outhouse**, Dublin’s publicly funded LGBTQ+ centre, brings several functions together: café and library space, social and peer-support groups, STI and rapid HIV testing, Garda liaison clinics, advice and referrals, coming-out support, and activism. It provides a route into the wider city community rather than limiting queer life to campus.

## Choosing the right first step

- For academic peer networks or queer scholarship, start with Pride in Research.
- For campus friendship and low-pressure social contact, use QSoc coffee hours or its inclusion scheme.
- For campaigns or student representation, contact TCDSU.
- For record changes, use Academic Registry and PAS according to the documentation route.
- For health, counselling, equality, misconduct, or legal questions, approach the specialist service shown above.
- For community outside Trinity, explore Outhouse and recurring groups rather than relying only on one-off nightlife listings.
