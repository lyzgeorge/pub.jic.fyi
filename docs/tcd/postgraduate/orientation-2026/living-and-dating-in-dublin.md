# Living and Dating in Dublin: A Practical Student Guide

> **Original title:** Living & Dating in Dublin PG
>
> **Session date:** 10 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=c7bb629c-6dea-4b7e-ad1f-b4b700ec41e8)

Dublin is a compact city centre surrounded by large suburbs, divided by the River Liffey into commonly described northside and southside areas. Much of student life is reachable on foot, by bicycle, or by public transport, but housing location strongly affects daily travel.

## Safety and urgent help

The session describes Dublin as generally safe while recommending ordinary city precautions: watch belongings, stay aware near ATMs and when entering accommodation, and avoid walking alone late at night where possible. An Garda Síochána is the Irish police service, commonly called “the Guards”. Call **112 or 999** in an emergency; on Trinity’s campus, call **01 896 1999**. Pearse Street Garda Station handles local non-emergency matters at **01 666 9000**.

## Healthcare and transport

Non-EU students need health insurance; EU students should use an EHIC where applicable. Insurance or an EHIC does not necessarily remove charges for accident-and-emergency attendance, ambulances, GPs, dentists, or physiotherapists. The session identifies St James’s and the Mater Misericordiae as nearby major hospitals and Dubdoc at St James’s as an out-of-hours GP service.

For daily travel, compare the Young Adult Leap Card for people under 26 with the Student Leap Card for eligible students aged 26 or above. Dublin Bus, Luas, DART, Dublin Bikes, taxis, and the TFI Live app form the main transport toolkit.

## Renting, shopping, and social life

Before renting, use Trinity’s Accommodation Advisory Service and consult Threshold and the Residential Tenancies Board for tenant rights. Keep written records and verify the property and agreement before transferring money.

Food costs vary greatly by shop type: Lidl and Aldi were presented as budget options; Tesco, SuperValu, and Dunnes Stores as mainstream supermarkets; convenience shops usually trade accessibility for higher prices. Student discounts can also reduce the cost of restaurants, culture, and travel.

Campus societies, clubs, Students’ Union events, the Pav, markets, performances, cafés, parks, museums, live music, and cinemas offer ways to meet people without making dating the sole route into a social network. When going to pubs, understand local habits such as buying rounds, decide in advance whether to drink, and plan the journey home.

## Dating, consent, and boundaries

Dating can begin through campus activities, public events, nightlife, or apps. Inclusive and LGBTQ+ venues and groups provide additional community routes. Whatever the setting, consent must be **ongoing, mutual, freely given, and for everyone**. It can be withdrawn, must be checked rather than assumed, and requires honest communication.

![Consent described as ongoing, mutual, freely given, and applicable to everyone](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/living-and-dating-in-dublin/consent-omfg.jpg)

Safer-sex planning includes open communication, bringing appropriate protection, knowing one’s status, and using contraception and STI-testing services. The recording points to free condoms and lubricant on campus, College Health, SH:24 home testing, rapid HIV testing through MPOWER, and the GUIDE Clinic at St James’s; availability and eligibility should be checked with the current provider.

## Safer first meetings

Treat online profiles as unverified until corroborated. Avoid giving an app match a full address, workplace, course details, or other information that makes you easy to locate. A brief video call or another established social profile may help verify identity, but neither guarantees safety.

![Smart dating tips: public meetings, telling a friend, protection, privacy, boundaries, profile checks, and awareness of power dynamics](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/living-and-dating-in-dublin/smart-dating-tips.jpg)

For a first meeting:

- meet in a public place and arrange independent transport;
- tell a friend or housemate where you are and when to expect contact;
- protect personal information and verify the profile where possible;
- set boundaries before and during the meeting;
- recognise power differences that may affect whether agreement is genuinely free;
- leave if the situation feels unsafe or consent is not respected.

Students affected by bullying, harassment, or sexual misconduct can contact Trinity’s Dignity, Respect & Consent Service for information and support.
