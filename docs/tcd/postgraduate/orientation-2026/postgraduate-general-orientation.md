# Postgraduate General Orientation

> **Original title:** PG General Orientation Monday
>
> **Session date:** 7 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=63000a95-73d6-4e3e-8898-b4b700e9440e)

The session maps the administrative, academic, wellbeing, campus, and library systems available to postgraduate students at Trinity College Dublin (TCD).

## Administrative foundations

Complete the basic administrative setup before more complex issues arise.

1. Register through `my.tcd.ie`, upload a photograph, and collect the T Card.
2. Read and retain the course handbook or research handbook. It is the primary reference for programme rules, assessment, and milestones.
3. Use the TCD email account when contacting university offices and include the eight-digit student number.
4. Keep the Irish address and telephone number current in `my.tcd.ie`.
5. Use formal and informal feedback channels to report what is working and what needs improvement.

### Where to direct a question

| Service | Scope | Access route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Postgraduate Advisory Service (PAS)** | Confidential frontline advice, advocacy, referrals, skills workshops, postgraduate support groups, and limited emergency financial assistance | `postgrad.support@tcd.ie`, `@tcdpgadvisory` |
| **Academic Registry** | Applications, registration, student records, fees, ID cards, examinations, graduation, and official documents | [ask.tcd.ie](https://ask.tcd.ie/) |
| **Dignity, Respect and Consent Service** | Disclosures, complaints, consent, sexual misconduct, bullying, and harassment | TCD dignity and respect service |
| **Equality, Diversity and Inclusion** | Inclusion, discrimination, equality training, and related resources | TCD equality service |
| **Policies Hub** | University procedures and policies, including student complaints, alcohol and drugs, mental health, and child protection | TCD policies website |

Academic Registry issues proof-of-registration and proof-of-address documents. The session states a normal processing time of two working days, rising to four working days during busy periods.

## Academic operating system

### Plan backwards from requirements

Postgraduate work requires earlier control of deadlines and expectations than last-minute task management.

- Record assessment dates, milestones, required materials, and administrative deadlines.
- Work backwards from each deadline into smaller deliverables.
- Set realistic goals and reserve time for research, reading, writing, and revision.
- Clarify academic expectations with lecturers or supervisors rather than inferring them.
- Identify required skills training early.

Students who need reasonable accommodations should register with the disAbility Service through `my.tcd.ie`. The session identifies the last Friday in October as the point before which the service aims to have Semester 1 examination accommodations ready; later applications remain possible but may not be implemented in time.

### Academic support services

| Service | Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Subject librarian** | Discipline-specific searching, databases, reading-list material, and research guidance |
| **Student Learning Development** | Workshops, online resources, individual consultations, academic writing, argument construction, and study methods |
| **Academic Writing Centre** | Feedback on short writing samples |
| **Careers Advisory Service** | Career planning, workshops, fairs, CV and interview clinics, and opportunities through MyCareer |
| **disAbility Service** | Needs assessment, assistive technology, academic support, and examination accommodations |

## Wellbeing as study infrastructure

Academic performance and wellbeing affect each other. The session treats exercise, food, hydration, sleep, mental health, spiritual support, and care during illness as part of a sustainable study system.

| Service | Provision |
| --- | --- |
| **College Health Centre** | Student primary care through doctors and nurses, sexual-health clinics, and physiotherapy for a reduced fee |
| **Student Counselling Services** | Individual counselling, support groups, workshops, postgraduate support groups, and online programmes |
| **Sports Centre** | Gym, pool, climbing wall, fitness classes, social sport, and outdoor facilities; students activate access with their T Card |
| **Chaplaincy** | Chaplains, links to multiple faith communities, prayer spaces, and support open to students of all faiths and none |

For out-of-hours listening and crisis support, the session lists **Text About It** at `50808` and the student-run **NiteLine** service.

## Participation and representation

Postgraduate participation extends beyond classes and research groups.

- **Class and school representatives** communicate postgraduate views to programmes and the university.
- **TCD Students' Union** is legally independent of the university and provides representation, welfare and education support, campaigns, and a postgraduate officer.
- **Global Room** supports international students with immigration information, peer support, and cultural events.
- **Sports clubs** cover approximately 50 activities and accept participants from beginner to competitive levels.
- **Student societies** cover academic disciplines, cultures, identities, publications, arts, and other interests.

A practical starting pattern is one society related to the student's discipline, one familiar activity, and one new activity. Postgraduate students can also influence clubs and societies by proposing events designed for their cohort.

## Campus systems

### IT access

The IT orientation sequence covers:

1. activating the computer account and email;
2. connecting to student Wi-Fi;
3. accessing coursework;
4. locating lectures and rooms;
5. obtaining supported software;
6. purchasing or configuring suitable hardware; and
7. requesting IT support.

### T Card

The **T Card** functions as student identification, building-access credential, printer and copier sign-in, and campus cashless-payment card. It can also be used to request student discounts on and off campus. The session states that T Card payment receives a 10% discount at campus catering outlets.

### Food and student spaces

Campus food options include the Buttery, Dining Hall, Forum, Perch, Students' Union café and shops, and the Pavilion. The Dining Hall and Buttery serve the same food in different settings; most catering outlets close before evening, while the Pavilion provides later hot-food options.

TCD does not have one central student centre. Instead, it provides a network of student spaces. The **Common Room** and **1937 Reading Room** are postgraduate-only spaces; other locations serve particular parts of campus.

![Map of postgraduate and general student spaces across Trinity's main campus.](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/postgraduate-general-orientation/student-spaces-map.jpg)

*Postgraduate and general student spaces are distributed across the campus rather than concentrated in one student centre.*

### Transport and sustainability

The session recommends walking, cycling, and public transport for Dublin travel. The TFI Live application provides route and fare information, while Young Adult and Student Leap Cards provide discounted public transport according to age and student status.

Campus sustainability practices include switching off unused equipment, using refill and reusable-cup facilities, sorting recycling, and returning eligible containers through reverse-vending machines. TCD sustainability maps identify facilities such as recycling points, water fountains, toilets, and sustainable food options.

## Safety

**SafeZone** connects users directly to TCD Security. It supports first aid, security assistance, emergencies, and check-ins for people working alone on campus. If a user checks in but does not check out, Security can follow up.

| Contact | Number |
| --- | --- |
| College Security | `01 896 1317` |
| Campus emergency | `01 896 1999` |

For emergencies on campus, the session directs students to SafeZone or the campus emergency number so that TCD Security can coordinate access for emergency responders. Fire alarms should be treated as genuine: leave by the marked emergency route rather than waiting for confirmation.

## Postgraduate study practices

### Build a peer network

Postgraduate study can become isolated, particularly during individual research. Maintain relationships inside and outside the programme, including classmates who understand the same deadlines and institutional context.

### Treat study as bounded full-time work

The session frames postgraduate study as broadly comparable to a full-time job. Late work may occasionally be necessary, but it should be exceptional rather than routine.

- Organize a repeatable schedule.
- Protect sufficient time for academic work without overcommitting.
- Maintain hobbies, exercise, friendships, and time away from the desk.
- Take a short break when work becomes unproductive, then return with a clearer view of the problem.
- Expect periods of frustration without treating them as evidence that the programme has failed.

### Navigate Irish academic culture

Irish social communication is often informal, but academic roles still carry expectations.

| Context | Convention described in the session |
| --- | --- |
| Corridor greeting | “How's it going?” commonly functions as a greeting rather than a request for a detailed status report |
| Social punctuality | Small delays may be accepted informally |
| Academic punctuality | Arrive on time or a few minutes early for lectures and staff meetings |
| Academic titles | Use `Dr`, `Professor`, or the relevant title until invited to use a first name |
| Informal language | Irish and Trinity-specific vocabulary may differ from other forms of English; ask when a term is unclear |

## Library system

TCD Library is a legal-deposit research library with approximately seven million physical items, 1.3 million ebooks, 150,000 electronic journals, and more than 3,000 study spaces.

### Spaces and access

- The **1937 Reading Room** is a 24-hour postgraduate study space with a computer lab, conversation room, kitchenette, and lockers.
- **Kinsella Hall** is another 24-hour study space available to all students.
- Dedicated postgraduate desks in the Lecky, Ussher, and Boland Libraries are allocated to PhD and master's-by-research students, not taught-master's students.
- Bookable group rooms and sensory-friendly spaces are distributed across the library system.

### Borrowing and retrieval

The session states that higher-degree students may borrow up to 50 general-lending books at once for 12 weeks, with one further 12-week renewal when no recall has been placed.

Use the catalogue shelfmark to find a print book. Because much of the collection is stored rather than on open shelves, the catalogue's **Click and Collect** function can route material to an appropriate service desk. On-shelf items are generally available within 24 hours; storage items are generally available on the next working day.

### Ebook distinction

| Ebook type | Access |
| --- | --- |
| Regular purchased ebook | Available remotely under the library's normal access conditions |
| UK electronic legal-deposit ebook | Available only on designated library computers because of legal-deposit restrictions |

Every discipline has a subject librarian. Library HITS sessions cover library basics, databases, plagiarism, referencing, reference management, literature reviews, academic writing, critical reading, and related research skills.

## Practical sequence

| Stage | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Initial setup | Register, collect the T Card, read the handbook, update contact details, and activate IT and Wi-Fi access |
| Academic planning | Record deadlines, identify expectations, meet the subject librarian, and register for required support or accommodations |
| Campus setup | Locate postgraduate spaces, install SafeZone, and learn the T Card, transport, food, and sustainability systems |
| During term | Use PAS or Academic Registry for the appropriate issue, maintain a bounded study schedule, and remain connected to peers |
| Research and assignments | Use catalogue retrieval, databases, subject librarians, Student Learning Development, and Library HITS training |
| When support is needed | Contact health, counselling, PAS, the Students' Union, Security, or another specialist service rather than waiting for the problem to escalate |
