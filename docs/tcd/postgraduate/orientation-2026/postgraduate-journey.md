# Your Postgraduate Journey at Trinity

> **Original title:** Your Postgraduate Journey
>
> **Session date:** 7 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=604a09fe-ed37-4f89-948b-b4b700e9d1f3)

Postgraduate education combines specialist study with entry into a wider scholarly community. The session explains how taught and research students can locate responsibility, develop as scholars, use institutional opportunities, and respond early when difficulties arise.

## Know which postgraduate route you are on

**Postgraduate taught (PGT)** programmes are structured around a course, modules, assessments, a course director, module coordinators, administrators, and a course committee. **Postgraduate research (PGR)** programmes centre on an individual research project supported by supervisors, a thesis committee, a research group or laboratory, and research-student coordinators.

Both routes sit within a School. The Director of Postgraduate Teaching and Learning has an oversight role, and a School appeal committee provides a formal route where applicable. The correct first contact depends on the issue: module questions normally go to the module coordinator; programme questions to the course team; research questions to a supervisor or thesis committee.

## Use a layered support network

Support is distributed rather than located in one office. The session identifies School handbooks, administrators, course directors, supervisors, thesis committees, the Director of Postgraduate Teaching and Learning, the Head of School, and the Dean of Graduate Studies. College-wide services include Academic Registry, the Postgraduate Advisory Support Service, Health and Counselling, Student Learning Development, Careers, Global Engagement, Disability Services, and the Students’ Union.

The practical principle is to start with the person closest to the issue and move through the structure when it remains unresolved. The handbook often states procedures and deadlines, while the Postgraduate Advisory Support Service offers independent guidance on academic processes and difficulties.

## Develop beyond the immediate programme

Trinity’s stated postgraduate vision includes excellent disciplinary education alongside opportunities to work across boundaries. The **Triple I** commitment describes three dimensions:

- **International:** learn in an international environment, engage with other cultures, apply international perspectives, and use them to create knowledge.
- **Intersectoral:** encounter organisations or practices beyond a single academic setting, apply that experience, and integrate sectoral perspectives.
- **Interdisciplinary:** interact with other disciplines, apply methods across fields, and combine approaches to generate new knowledge.

![Triple I student-engagement model showing international, intersectoral, and interdisciplinary development from exposure to extension](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/postgraduate-journey/triple-i-model.jpg)

The model moves from **exposure**, through **experience** and **application**, to **extension**: students first encounter another context, work within it, apply what they learn, and ultimately integrate it into new scholarship.

For researchers, development also includes methods, communication, teaching, career planning, networking, and professional skills. The session encourages students to use structured development opportunities rather than viewing the thesis or taught curriculum as the whole postgraduate experience.

## Scholarship and academic integrity

Scholarship requires intellectual independence, transparent use of sources, and adherence to programme and College rules. Students should read their handbook and relevant regulations rather than relying on informal assumptions about assessment, progression, extensions, or thesis requirements.

Generative AI must be used competently, critically, ethically, and responsibly.

![Slide directing students to Trinity statements and high-level principles for postgraduate researchers using generative AI](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/postgraduate-journey/generative-ai-guidance.jpg)

The session highlights four principles:

| Principle | Practical implication |
| --- | --- |
| Transparency and honesty | Disclose use when required and do not misrepresent generated material as independent work. |
| Responsibility and accountability | The student remains responsible for accuracy, quality, authorship, and compliance. |
| Respect | Protect confidential, personal, copyrighted, and research-sensitive material. |
| Innovation | Explore useful tools within ethical, disciplinary, and programme boundaries. |

Specific permission can differ by assignment, course, discipline, or stage of doctoral work. The applicable assessment instructions, College statement, and doctoral guidance take priority over generic assumptions.

## Maximise the chance of success

![Slide summarising five success practices: plan time, know regulations, read the handbook, maintain academic integrity, and seek support](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/postgraduate-journey/maximizing-success.jpg)

Five habits underpin successful progression:

1. **Plan time:** map major deadlines and leave room for revision, research delays, and personal demands.
2. **Know the regulations:** understand progression, assessment, submission, absence, and appeal procedures.
3. **Read the handbook:** use the local document that translates broad regulations into programme practice.
4. **Protect academic integrity:** cite accurately and clarify rules before using unfamiliar tools or assistance.
5. **Seek support:** raise uncertainty or difficulty before it becomes a crisis.

## When the journey becomes difficult

Setbacks may be academic, supervisory, administrative, financial, health-related, or personal. Early communication preserves more options. Formal requests—such as going off books—normally proceed through the course director for taught students or the supervisor for researchers. If the first contact cannot resolve the matter, the Director of Postgraduate Teaching and Learning is a further School-level route.

The Postgraduate Advisory Support Service and Students’ Union can help students understand options independently. Asking for help is part of managing a postgraduate programme, not evidence that a student does not belong.

## Action checklist

- Identify whether your issue belongs at module, course, supervisory, School, or College level.
- Save the current handbook and record key deadlines.
- Meet the people responsible for your programme or research oversight.
- Plan for scholarly, professional, international, intersectoral, or interdisciplinary development.
- Check the exact AI and academic-integrity rules for each piece of work.
- Raise difficulties early and escalate through the defined support structure when necessary.
