# Reasonable Accommodations and Disability Support

> **Original title:** Applying for Reasonable Exam Accommodations with the disAbility Service
>
> **Session date:** 8 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=b6336877-b638-4188-90cb-b4ba00b06f15)

A reasonable accommodation removes or reduces a substantial disadvantage caused by disability so that a student has equitable opportunity to participate. It is not identical treatment: the arrangement is individual, course-aware, and agreed with the student.

![Slide contrasting equality with equity and listing examples such as extra exam time, smaller venues, recording, respite spaces, assistive technology, and occupational therapy](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/reasonable-accommodations/equity-and-accommodations.jpg)

## Who can contact the service

The disAbility Service works across broad disability categories and with both confirmed and suspected disabilities. The session explicitly says that difficulty obtaining a current diagnostic report should not prevent a student from making contact. Documentation can be reviewed with the service; non-English documents may need translation.

Registration leads to an assigned contact, but students may approach other team members. Needs can change over a programme, so accommodations can be revisited rather than fixed permanently at the first meeting.

## How registration works

![Slide showing the route through my.tcd.ie to the My Disability Service tab](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/reasonable-accommodations/registration-route.jpg)

The recorded route is to sign into `my.tcd.ie`, open **My Disability Service** under the plus menu, select a broad category, accept relevant policies, provide available documentation and impact information, indicate accommodation needs, and submit.

The service may implement straightforward exam arrangements from the application and meet the student subsequently. A **needs assessment** explores the individual course, placements, research or taught structure, barriers, and less-standard arrangements.

Exam accommodations have a deadline each semester because information must reach the examinations team before timetables and venues are finalised. The recording gave 30 October 2026 for that semester; future dates must be checked. Late contact is still encouraged, but implementation becomes harder.

## Examples of accommodation

Depending on demonstrated impact, arrangements may include additional exam time; group, low-distraction, or individual venues; ear protection; a computer, scribe, or dictation system; permission to record lectures; flexible deadlines; assistive technology; respite and sensory spaces; placement or viva arrangements; and accessibility changes to learning environments.

Additional time is not prescribed for one use: the student uses it according to need. Individual rooms are reserved for cases requiring them; many students may prefer a lower-distraction group venue. Non-standard requests require discussion with the student and department.

## LENS report, consent, and privacy

The **Learning Educational Needs Summary (LENS)** communicates agreed accommodations. Disability information is treated as sensitive data. Registration allows the service to hold supplied information, but does not automatically disclose everything to a supervisor or department. The student confirms the report and gives consent before accommodations are shared.

If a department has difficulty implementing an agreed measure, bring the disAbility Service back into the conversation rather than negotiating alone.

## Support beyond formal accommodations

![Wheel showing support with self-care, placements, deadlines, exams, meeting other students, and managing environments](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/reasonable-accommodations/support-areas.jpg)

One-to-one and group support can address routine, workload, deadlines, sensory environments, self-care, placements, careers, and connection with peers. Occupational therapy focuses on practical participation; assistive-technology staff advise on tools; sensory and respite spaces offer lower-stimulation options. The service also connects with College Health and Counselling where appropriate, while diagnosis and medical treatment remain separate functions.

## Action checklist

1. Contact the service even if documentation is incomplete or old.
2. Register through My Disability Service and describe functional impact, not only diagnosis.
3. Check the current exam-accommodation deadline immediately.
4. Review the proposed LENS report and control consent to share it.
5. Ask for a needs assessment where the course, placement, viva, or barrier requires individual planning.
6. Revisit arrangements when needs change.
7. Report implementation barriers to the service.
8. Use practical, technological, peer, sensory, health, and counselling support as relevant.
