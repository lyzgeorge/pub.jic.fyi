# Research Practice at Trinity: Evidence, Ethics, Profiles, and Funding

> **Original title:** Research Tips with the Experts
>
> **Session date:** 8 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=e108f6d9-1568-4619-8467-b4b700ea3427)

Good research depends on more than a strong topic. It requires a reproducible evidence strategy, compliance with policy and ethics, a visible record of outputs, and advance planning for resources and funding.

## Build an evidence process with the Library

Subject librarians connect disciplinary expertise with databases, search design, source evaluation, reference management, and evidence synthesis.

![Slide describing the subject librarian as a first contact for quality evidence, consultations, research tools, and systematic or scoping review support](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/research-tips/subject-librarian.jpg)

Contact the librarian early, particularly for systematic or scoping reviews. A useful consultation includes the research question, key concepts and synonyms, known papers, inclusion boundaries, likely databases, and the intended evidence product. Record queries, dates, filters, and decisions so that the search can be explained and updated.

Library subject pages, research guides, and tutorials support database choice, advanced searching, citation, copyright, research data, and literature-review methods. The librarian advises the process; the researcher remains responsible for disciplinary judgement.

## Treat policy as part of research design

Trinity’s research policies cover good research practice, integrity, ethics, data protection, intellectual property, authorship, open research, data management, export or security issues, and other field-specific requirements. Relevant policies should be identified before data collection, not consulted only after a problem.

Good research practice includes accurate records, transparent methods, responsible data stewardship, appropriate authorship and acknowledgement, management of conflicts, and correction of error. Supervisors and research teams should agree how evidence, code, data, consent records, and decisions will be stored.

## Ethics approval precedes the research activity

Research involving people, personal data, sensitive material, animals, or other defined risks may require ethics review. Determine the responsible committee and whether data-protection review is also needed before recruitment, collection, or analysis begins.

![Slide directing postgraduates to Trinity ethics guidance and the REAMS research-ethics application system](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/research-tips/ethics-approval.jpg)

Trinity uses **REAMS**, the Research Ethics Application Management System, for relevant applications. Postgraduates are registered to access it. Read the committee guidance and prepare protocol, methods, participant information, consent materials, data handling, risk assessment, recruitment wording, and supporting documents consistently.

Approval is not a one-time shield. Amendments, unexpected events, changes to data use, or new methods may require further review. Conduct only the activity covered by the approval.

## Maintain a research profile

A research profile makes expertise, affiliations, outputs, projects, supervision, and collaboration interests discoverable. Trinity’s Research Support System (RSS) supplies institutional research data and can support profile publishing. Accurate records also feed reporting and help potential collaborators, media, funders, and students find relevant work.

Create the profile early, use stable identifiers such as ORCID where supported, keep names and affiliations consistent, and update publications, grants, activities, and keywords. A profile is a maintained research record, not only a promotional biography.

## Treat funding as skill and infrastructure

Funding can provide stipend or salary, research resources, training, travel, collaboration, and time. Applying also develops proposal writing, budgeting, project design, and the ability to explain significance and feasibility. Across career stages, awards can support independence, networks, project management, teams, supervision, and leadership.

Start from the research need rather than a generic search for money. Define stage, discipline, eligibility, costs, location, partners, and timeline. Work backwards from the deadline for institutional approval, references, costing, ethics or data planning, and revision.

The session points to postgraduate and postdoctoral opportunities, Research Ireland schemes, the weekly **Research Focus** newsletter, the **Research Professional** database and alerts, and Research Development Office training. Calls and dates change, so use current listings.

## Integrated workflow

1. Convert the topic into a searchable question and consult the subject librarian.
2. Record a transparent evidence-search strategy.
3. Identify policies, data obligations, and ethics routes before collecting material.
4. Obtain approval and track conditions or amendments.
5. Keep an accurate RSS/ORCID-linked profile of outputs and activity.
6. Map resource needs to suitable calls and begin before the deadline window.
7. Use grant writing as a way to sharpen aims, methods, feasibility, and impact.
