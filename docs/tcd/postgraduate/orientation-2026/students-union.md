# Trinity Students’ Union: Representation, Support, and Participation

> **Original title:** Your Students' Union
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Embed.aspx?id=320d6034-8a94-4dc5-96f5-b4b700eb4d94)

Trinity College Dublin Students’ Union (TCDSU) is the representative body for the university’s students. Founded in 1968, it reported about 23,000 members in 2026. Its independence allows elected representatives to advocate for positions set by students rather than by College management.

## What the union does

TCDSU combines four functions:

- **representation and advocacy:** raising student issues within Trinity, with other Dublin institutions, nationally through the wider student movement, and directly with government;
- **casework and navigation:** helping individuals understand academic, welfare, equality, or accommodation problems and reach the appropriate College service;
- **practical services:** operating an accommodation advisory service, two shops, a low-cost device-repair service, and a café;
- **community and events:** organising social, cultural, welfare, and non-alcohol events through Trinity Ents.

Recent campaigning cited in the session included access to education for Palestinian students, university divestment, resistance to fee and rent increases, a continuing rent freeze in university-owned accommodation, and implementation of disability accommodations.

## Casework: where individual problems become actionable

Three full-time officers undertake casework. The Education Officer primarily handles undergraduate academic matters and supports postgraduate academic cases. The Welfare and Equality Officer handles welfare and accommodation concerns. The Graduate Officer—the first full-time holder of this role—supports both taught and research postgraduates across academic and welfare issues.

Caseworkers may advocate directly, explain procedures, or signpost another service. The union is therefore a useful first contact when a student does not yet know which office owns a problem. It does not replace specialist services, but helps students identify a workable next step.

For matters outside the union’s remit, the session highlighted:

- the **Senior Tutor** for difficult assessment deferrals, extensions, or unresolved tutor issues;
- **Academic Registry** for student records, replacement ID cards, fees, and payments;
- the **Careers Advisory Service** for employment guidance and vacancies;
- **Student Learning Development** for academic work and study practices;
- the **Library** for collections, loans, and due dates.

## Accommodation and everyday services

The Accommodation Advisory Service publishes listings that the union says it vets before approval and gives landlords a price guide before listings appear. Students can also seek advice from union officers about accommodation problems.

The union’s campus shops sell food, stationery, merchandise, equipment, and required TCD lab coats. Its repair service charges students for parts rather than ordinary commercial labour costs. The SU Café in Goldsmith Hall offers inexpensive food and coffee. These services are practical rather than representative, but are intended to reduce day-to-day friction and expense.

## A layered system of representation

Representation moves from a course-level concern toward School, Faculty, postgraduate, or union-wide decision-making.

| Role or body | Scope | Main function |
|---|---|---|
| Class representative | One year of one course | Raises shared academic problems, communicates with course administration, helps organise the cohort, and participates in union bodies |
| Faculty assembly | Arts, STEM, or Health Sciences | Discusses issues specific to the Faculty; each assembly meets four times a year |
| Postgraduate Forum | Postgraduate students | Concentrates on postgraduate issues |
| School convenor | One of Trinity’s Schools | Represents a whole School and may sit on School committees |
| Faculty convenor | Entire Faculty | Escalates and coordinates Faculty-level representation |
| Part-time officer | A defined constituency or issue | Leads work in areas such as international students, the environment, off-campus students, taught postgraduates, or postgraduate research |
| Union council | Union-wide | Sets policy and decides how the union operates; the session described seven meetings per year |

A class representative is the lowest-threshold formal role. Representatives can aggregate problems that are hard to solve individually—for example, clustered deadlines—and bring them to course administration. The session cited law representatives securing a clearer lecture-recording policy as an example of collective representation producing a concrete change.

## Postgraduate routes into the union

Postgraduates can participate at several levels:

1. stand as a class representative;
2. attend the Postgraduate Forum or relevant Faculty assembly;
3. seek election as a postgraduate School convenor;
4. run for the taught-postgraduate or postgraduate-research part-time officer role;
5. apply for committee positions, including the postgraduate place on the Education Committee;
6. contact the Graduate Officer for individual casework or wider postgraduate concerns.

The taught-postgraduate and postgraduate-research officers represent their respective constituencies across Trinity. These roles involve more sustained union governance than class representation and include participation in a forum concerned with the operational detail of the union.

## Full-time elected officers

Sabbatical officers take a year away from or after study to work full time. The President leads campaigns, acts as a principal public representative, oversees operations and finances, and helps route queries. Other portfolios cover education, welfare and equality, communications and marketing, events, the Irish language, and postgraduate affairs.

The union operates bilingually in English and Irish. The Irish Language Officer promotes equal status for both languages, supports Irish-language activity, and works with societies and campaigns. The session also identified conversational Irish classes offered through Trinity and through a union–society partnership, with beginner and intermediate options; demand was expected to be high.

## Events as participation and welfare infrastructure

Trinity Ents runs daytime and evening programmes ranging from pub quizzes and music events to crafts and other alcohol-free activities. Events are not only entertainment: they provide a low-friction way to form social connections. Welfare team members in red jackets attend events so that students can find immediate help when something goes wrong.

## A practical way to use the union

- For an individual academic, welfare, equality, or accommodation problem, contact the relevant casework officer or the Graduate Officer.
- For a shared course problem, document the pattern and raise it through the class representative.
- If no representative exists, consider standing: the role is designed as an accessible entry point.
- Escalate School- or Faculty-wide issues through convenors and assemblies.
- Follow union communications for elections, committee vacancies, events, services, and short-lived offers.
- When the institutional vocabulary or responsible office is unclear, ask the union to route the query rather than leaving it unresolved.
