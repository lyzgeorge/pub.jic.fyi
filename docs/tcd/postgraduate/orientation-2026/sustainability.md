# Sustainability at Trinity

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Sustainability
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=1fa84dff-1ee5-49a5-9ffc-b4ba00b23125)

Trinity treats sustainability as a connection between a healthy planet and healthy people. Its institutional approach links climate change, biodiversity loss, and health across four domains: education, research, operations, and community participation.

## Strategic framework

The strategy sets three headline outcomes: net zero greenhouse-gas emissions by 2040, nature-positive activity by 2030, and improved physical and mental health by 2030. These outcomes are pursued through education, research, operations, and community rather than through isolated events.

![Diagram connecting Trinity's climate, biodiversity, and health targets with education, research, operations, and community](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/sustainability/strategy-framework.jpg)

The framework matters because operational carbon is only one part of university impact. Teaching shapes future professional decisions; research creates knowledge but also consumes energy and materials; procurement affects distant ecosystems; and campus choices influence health and participation.

The recording states that Trinity, as an Irish public-sector body, is working toward a 51% emissions reduction by 2030 and has set its own net-zero target for 2040. Net zero requires deep emissions reduction, with residual emissions addressed separately. For biodiversity, Trinity aims to protect, manage, or restore 30% of its land for nature by 2030 while improving habitat quality.

## Education and research

Education for Sustainable Development fellows audit curricula, help staff connect sustainability with different disciplines, develop modules, and train educators. The *Enacting Sustainable Development* module introduces systems thinking, planetary boundaries, ecology, biodiversity, and climate to business students. Student auditors also participate in the recurring Responsible Futures review of curriculum and institutional practice.

Research contributes through climate, biodiversity, and water programmes, but research practice itself has an environmental footprint. Laboratories use substantial energy, plastics, equipment, and other resources. Trinity's Green Labs programme works with principal investigators, researchers, and technical teams to reduce that impact through assessment and accreditation.

## Sustainable campus operations

Operational work is organised around five connected ideas:

- **Nature-positive campus:** restore habitats, improve biodiversity literacy, and involve the community in activities such as tours and tree planting.
- **Climate-smart campus:** retrofit buildings, replace fossil-fuel heat, and follow a decarbonisation pathway toward 2040.
- **Active campus:** make walking, cycling, and public transport easier and safer.
- **Circular campus:** prevent waste by sharing, repairing, redistributing, and reusing materials before recycling or disposal.
- **Responsible campus:** consider impacts from events, food, procurement, laboratories, infrastructure, and supply chains.

### Travel

Students can compare walking, cycling, and public-transport routes through TFI Live and Trinity's map hub. Young Adult or Student Leap Cards may provide public-transport discounts under current eligibility rules. Campus facilities include bicycle parking, while shared-bike services and travel challenges provide additional options. Current discounts, facilities, and safety arrangements should be checked before relying on them.

![Active Campus slide listing walking, public transport, Leap Card, bicycle parking, bike sharing, and participation routes](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/sustainability/active-campus.jpg)

A bicycle should not be treated as disposable when a student leaves. The session describes routes for returning usable abandoned bicycles to students or community partners. Students who are unfamiliar with cycling in Dublin can ask the sustainability team about training or a buddy arrangement.

### Food, events, and buildings

A decarbonisation strategy prioritises buildings where investment can reduce emissions while improving comfort. A green-events plan targets avoidable impacts such as disposable cups. Sustainable and healthy food work includes more plant-forward choices, affordability, procurement, and measuring kitchen waste so menus and production quantities can be adjusted.

## Circular use of resources

The circular model keeps useful goods in use. Trinity's Trash to Treasure initiative collects clothing, electrical goods, household items, bedding, and other possessions during move-out; items are sorted, cleaned where necessary, and offered to incoming students. This reduces incineration and helps with living costs.

![Circular Campus slide showing move-out waste, cleaned bedding, reuse sales, and the deposit-return scheme](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/sustainability/circular-campus.jpg)

The deposit-return scheme applies the same principle to eligible drink containers. Student groups can organise collection and direct returned deposits toward an activity or cause, subject to current scheme rules.

## How students can participate

Participation ranges from changing routine behaviour to contributing to institutional decisions:

- join the Green Campus Committee or, for laboratory work, the Green Labs committee;
- volunteer for curriculum auditing or sustainability action weeks;
- participate in the urban garden, biodiversity tours, tree planting, transport surveys, and travel challenges;
- propose activities and provide feedback on the student engagement plan;
- follow internship opportunities involving mapping, carbon footprints, circularity, or engagement;
- nominate eligible work for the Sustainability Leadership Awards;
- attend climate-conference activity virtually or through opportunities advertised by Trinity.

Dates and recruitment cycles mentioned in the 2026 recording are time-specific. Check current Trinity Sustainability announcements before planning around them.

## Everyday action

![Slide summarising everyday actions: use less, share, travel sustainably, avoid disposables, choose more plant-based food, reuse and repair, switch off, and speak up](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/sustainability/everyday-actions.jpg)

A useful hierarchy is to reduce demand first, share and reuse what already exists, repair before replacement, and recycle only after higher-value options are exhausted. On campus this can mean carrying a reusable bottle or cup, using water stations, switching off unused equipment, choosing lower-impact travel and food, and returning reusable items rather than abandoning them.

Individual action is most effective when infrastructure supports it. Trinity's sustainability maps identify facilities such as water stations, recycling points, bicycle parking, bio-havens, quiet spaces, and other amenities. Students can also report barriers and ask the institution to make sustainable choices easier.

## Practical checklist

1. Find Trinity Sustainability's current website, maps, newsletter, and announcements.
2. Choose one transport, food, energy, or reuse habit that is realistic to maintain.
3. Donate or redistribute usable belongings when moving rather than leaving them behind.
4. If working in a laboratory, ask about Green Labs practices and accreditation.
5. Join a committee, audit, action week, garden, survey, or project relevant to your interests.
6. Verify current dates, discounts, targets, and opportunities rather than relying on the recording's calendar.
7. Give feedback where campus systems make lower-impact choices unnecessarily difficult.
