# Trinity Sport: Clubs, Recreation, Facilities, and Inclusive Participation

> **Original title:** Let's Talk About... Trinity Sport
>
> **Session date:** 9 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=5a2ae120-e724-44f7-a713-b4c000d62bad)

Trinity Sport provides several ways to be active: competitive or developmental sports clubs, relaxed recreational programmes, fitness classes, bookable facilities, and support for high-performance and inclusive participation. These routes serve different goals, from competition and skill development to wellbeing, friendship, and occasional activity.

## Choose between clubs and recreational activity

**Sports clubs** are organised groups built around particular sports. Trinity has more than 45, including rowing, Gaelic games, football, rugby, swimming, and fencing. Many offer beginner pathways as well as opportunities for experienced, competitive, or international-level athletes. The Trinity Sport Union supports club governance, administration, committees, and new-member participation.

Students can review club descriptions online and join through Clubforce using their Trinity email account. Club representatives and the Trinity Sport Union committee can help with questions about membership or involvement.

**Social sport and participation programmes** are less formal. Their emphasis is regular activity, meeting people, and enjoyment rather than sustained competition. They are intended to remain accessible even to students who do not think of themselves as sporty or who disliked school physical education.

Recreational provision falls into four broad forms:

- leagues with a light competitive element;
- courses that teach a sport or physical activity from the basics;
- relaxed recurring social programmes;
- community events, including campus runs for staff and students.

The programme described a 3 km run in semester one and a 5 km run in semester two. Dates, schedules, and programme availability can change, so the current Trinity Sport calendar should be treated as authoritative.

## Use the app as the central access point

The Trinity Sport app is the main gateway for sport and physical activity. It links club and participation information, supports activation of Sports Centre membership, and allows bookings for fitness classes and facilities. It is available through the Apple App Store and Google Play.

Fitness provision includes wellness, circuit, and spin classes at different times. Students can select a suitable class in the app and book directly. The Trinity Sport website and student sport guide provide fuller programme and contact details.

## Understand membership before paying

Most Trinity students receive Sports Centre membership without an additional charge, including access to the gym, pool, and some other amenities. A fee may apply to certain visiting students depending on their home institution. The recording quoted **€76.50 per semester**, while also mentioning a full-academic-year option.

Visiting students should not assume either free or paid access from the quoted figure alone. Sports Centre reception can check the individual account and confirm whether a fee applies; current published prices and terms should override the recording.

## Facilities extend beyond the main campus centre

The main Sports Centre is at the far end of the central campus and is the base for many activities. Its facilities include a 25-metre pool, gym and exercise studios, a climbing wall, spin and wellness studios, and steam and sauna rooms.

Other central-campus facilities include rugby and tennis areas, a futsal court at Botany Bay, College Park, and facilities described at Printing House Square, including squash courts, a handball alley, and an Olympic target range. Off-campus training locations include Islandbridge Boathouse for rowing and boat clubs, Iveagh Sports Grounds in Crumlin, and Santry Sports Grounds.

Participation does not always require joining a club or structured course. Students can use the app to book facilities—for example, a hall for badminton with friends—and Trinity Sport may provide equipment for the session.

## Performance and inclusion have dedicated support

High-performance athletes can access a sports scholarship programme and related university and Trinity Sport support. Eligibility and application information are available through Trinity Sport's current web pages and contacts.

Participation initiatives include Women in Sport programmes, Movember and Pride campaigns, free recreational events and classes, and adapted sport. The recording announced a wheelchair basketball pilot and emphasised that students who need accommodations or additional support should contact Trinity Sport. These initiatives connect physical activity with social health, wellbeing, equality, and belonging rather than treating sport only as competition.

## Practical route to getting involved

1. Browse current clubs, social programmes, classes, and contacts on the Trinity Sport website or student sport guide.
2. Install the Trinity Sport app and activate the membership attached to the student account.
3. Use Clubforce with a Trinity email account when joining a sports club.
4. Check the programme calendar for times, locations, trial periods, and booking requirements.
5. Ask Sports Centre reception to confirm visiting-student fees or account access.
6. Contact Trinity Sport early if accommodations, adapted activity, or high-performance support are needed.
