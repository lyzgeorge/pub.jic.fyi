# Career Development at Trinity

> **Original title:** Your Career
>
> **Session date:** 10 September 2026
>
> **Source:** [Panopto recording](https://tcd.cloud.panopto.eu/Panopto/Pages/Viewer.aspx?id=05de32b6-ba3c-4729-bb82-b4b700ebf9da)

Career development is an iterative process of understanding yourself, exploring options, building skills, creating relationships and opportunities, and choosing a workable balance between career and life. Trinity supports students at different stages—from having no fixed plan to targeting a defined profession—and remains available to recent graduates for one year after graduation.

![Map My Future career-development framework](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/your-career/map-my-future.jpg)

The **Map My Future** framework connects six tasks: start from current interests and circumstances, understand external influences, explore recruitment and workplace cultures, develop skills, create opportunities through people and experiences, and protect a sustainable career–life balance.

## Choose the correct careers service

Trinity has two routes. Postgraduate students in Trinity Business School use its Careers & Community team and the **12Twenty** platform. Other postgraduate and PhD students use the university-wide Trinity Careers Service and **MyCareer**. The distinction matters because appointments, events and resources are managed through the relevant platform.

## Use MyCareer as the working hub

![MyCareer functions: appointments, opportunities and queries](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/your-career/mycareer-platform.jpg)

MyCareer provides the event calendar, one-to-one guidance, CV and LinkedIn clinics, interview practice, vacancies, employer and postgraduate-course research, and a route for short questions. The service also offers career fairs, employer events, psychometric tools, mentoring, downloadable application guides and online interview practice. A targeted search developed with a careers consultant is often more useful than subscribing to large volumes of generic vacancy alerts.

Networking is not limited to direct job requests. Career-connection events and Trinity's alumni mentoring group let students learn how graduates navigated decisions, industries and transitions. These conversations remain useful even when the graduate's job is not an exact match.

## Tailored postgraduate routes

International students should separate career guidance from immigration advice. Trinity Global Room and the Immigration Support Officer are the appropriate sources for visas, immigration status and work permissions; Careers can help translate those constraints into a job-search plan.

PhD candidates can take or audit 5- and 10-ECTS career-development modules. Both combine practical skills, reflective work and peer learning across disciplines; the 10-ECTS route adds a self-sourced work-experience component. Topics include networking, strengths, emotional demands of doctoral study, CVs and interviews.

## Trinity Career Skills Award

The award turns activity outside the curriculum into evidence of employability. It recognises current and previous experience—such as volunteering, caring, part-time work, societies, teaching, projects and mentoring—when students identify the transferable skills gained.

![Three components of the Trinity Career Skills Award](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/your-career/career-skills-award-components.jpg)

Every level combines three categories: employer-partnership activities; development beyond the classroom; and experiential learning. Higher levels require more activities and a short reflection on what was learned.

![Participation, Achievement and Exceptional Career Skills Award levels](/assets/tcd/postgraduate/orientation-2026/your-career/career-skills-award-levels.jpg)

The award is flexible rather than all-or-nothing. Students register through the MyCareer pathway, record qualifying activities and complete the requirements by the published deadline. Current dates, partners and eligible activities should be checked in MyCareer because schedules change.

## Practical starting sequence

1. Confirm whether MyCareer or Business School 12Twenty is your service route.
2. Log in, review the calendar and book one useful appointment or event.
3. Describe your present position before selecting vacancies: interests, constraints, strengths and uncertainties.
4. Use clinics and practice tools for a specific CV, LinkedIn profile, application or interview.
5. Add one human connection through a fair, employer event, alumni conversation or mentoring group.
6. If useful, register for the Career Skills Award and map existing experience to its three categories.
7. For immigration or work-permission rules, verify the current position with Trinity Global Room or the relevant official authority.
